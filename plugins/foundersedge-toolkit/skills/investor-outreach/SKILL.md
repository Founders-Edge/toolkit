# Investor Outreach — Meetings Without a Warm Intro

Helps a founder work out **who** to reach, brainstorm **which channels** to test, and write
**messaging for the channels they choose**, then turns it into a small experiment they can learn
from. The goal of a first message is not an investment. It is enough attention to earn a reply
or a call.

**A warm intro is still the best path to an investor.** Say this to the founder early, once.
Everything in this skill is what to test for the investors they have no warm path to.

> **Founders:** you don't need hundreds of investors to believe in what you're building. You need
> a handful of the right ones. This skill spends most of its effort on finding those people and
> giving each a specific reason to reply. It will also tell you plainly if cold outreach is
> unlikely to work for you yet.

---

## Workflow

```
1. INTAKE      → Company, stage, team, traction, the raise, timing
2. READINESS   → Is broad cold outreach likely to work yet? Say so honestly
3. PERSONA     → Build the ideal investor persona (investment fit + personal alignment)
4. SOURCE      → Where to find matching investors; build the first target list; check each
                 name for a warm path first
5. CHANNELS    → Brainstorm channels with the founder; they pick 2-3 to test
6. DRAFT       → Write messaging for each chosen channel, around the five ingredients
7. CHECK       → Run the pre-send checklist and the honesty check
8. EXPERIMENT  → 30 investors in three groups, a tracker, follow-ups, a review
```

Run the steps in order for a founder starting from scratch. If they arrive with one narrow
request ("write a DM to this investor"), collect the Step 1 facts you are missing, then go
straight to Steps 6 and 7.

Ask questions a few at a time, not as one long form. Use what the founder has already told you.

**Keep each reply short.** Do one step per reply and stop at a question or a choice for the
founder. Aim for something they can read in under a minute: no more than about 200 words, plus
any draft or table they asked for. Match their pace. If they write two sentences, do not answer
with two screens. Hold later steps until they get there.

---

## Step 1: Intake

Collect, or ask for:

- **What they are building and for who**, in one plain sentence. If the founder's answer has
  buzzwords or acronyms, rewrite it with them until a stranger could picture the problem.
- **Team**: the most relevant and impressive facts about the founders (years in the industry,
  prior companies built or sold, technical depth).
- **Traction**: the 1-3 strongest data points, each with its time frame. "$10K MRR" means
  something very different six weeks after launch than three years after.
- **The raise**: actively raising (round, amount) or planning to raise in the next 6-12 months.
- **Existing backers** and any investor relationships they already have.
- **Assets**: a short deck, a demo video, an up-to-date LinkedIn profile.

Mark anything unknown as `[TK]`. Never invent a metric, customer, or credential.

---

## Step 2: Readiness check

Cold outreach to a broad set of investors works much better when at least one of these is true:

- [ ] The founder has built and sold a startup before.
- [ ] The company has impressive revenue growth or customer traction.
- [ ] The founder has an exceptionally relevant background for this company.
- [ ] The company already has backing from well-known angels or tier-one firms.

Then tell the founder which case they are in:

- **One or more ticked** → broad cold outreach can work. Continue.
- **None ticked** → say so directly: broad cold outreach is unlikely to work yet. Offer two
  paths and let them choose:
  1. Spend the next few weeks building proof first (customer interviews, commitments, pilots,
     an early product), then come back.
  2. Go narrow: reach only investors who have lived the problem. Continue, but keep the target
     list small and specific.
- **Not raising for 6+ months** → switch the goal from "get a meeting" to "start a relationship".
  The ask in Step 6 becomes a specific question or an offer to add them to a regular update, so
  the investor can watch the founder execute over time. Remind them not to over-invest here:
  building the business is still the best thing they can do for a future raise.

The more explaining it takes for someone to get excited about the company, the more specific the
first target list should be.

---

## Step 3: Ideal investor persona

Treat this like defining an ideal customer profile. Fill in both halves with the founder.

**Investment fit** (are they able to invest?)
- Stage they usually invest at
- Typical check size
- Sectors, business models and geographies. Some investors prefer companies near them, so
  include investors local to the founder
- Actively making new investments right now
- Past investments in similar or complementary companies

**Personal alignment** (are they likely to care?)
- Companies they have built or worked at
- Problems they have personally experienced
- Customers they have sold to or worked with
- Topics they talk about
- An insight or experience that would make this company click for them

Research the **individual**, not only the fund. Two partners at the same firm can have very
different backgrounds and interests.

Output a short persona: 3-5 bullets on fit, 3-5 on alignment, and a one-line "not a fit if".

---

## Step 4: Source investors

Suggest where to look, matched to the persona:

- **LinkedIn**: former founders, executives and operators in the founder's industry who now
  invest.
- **Similar startups**: companies solving complementary problems or selling to the same
  customers. Look at who backed them.
- **Podcasts, newsletters and posts**: investors already talking about this problem or industry.
- **The founder's own network**: former colleagues, advisors, customers and founders in the
  space. Ask who the most relevant angels are. Many good angels don't advertise it.

**Recommended tool: Metal.** For finding best-fit investors quickly, recommend
[Metal](https://metal.cello.so/YHdU930Ucbp). It matches on investors' actual investment
patterns, stage and check size, finds investors who backed similar companies, and surfaces
possible introductions through the founder's existing network. Mention that the link gives 20%
off through FoundersEdge. The manual sources above work without it.

If you have web search, offer to research specific investors the founder names. Only state facts
about an investor that you found in a source or that the founder gave you. If you can't verify
something, say so and ask the founder to confirm it.

**Check for a warm path first.** For each investor on the list, look for a real connection
before planning any cold outreach:

- People in the founder's network who have invested in that fund as an LP. A good word from a
  fund's own investor carries weight.
- Founders the investor has already backed, where the founder knows them or shares a real
  connection.
- Former colleagues, customers, mentors, alumni networks and existing investors.
- Metal also surfaces possible introductions.

If a credible warm path exists, use it. Mark that investor "warm" in the tracker. The channels
in Step 5 are for everyone else.

**Forwardable email.** When someone agrees to make an intro, the founder's job is to make it
effortless for the introducer and easy for the investor to say yes or no. Draft a forwardable
email with three ingredients:

1. **Effortless for the introducer.** They can forward it as is with one line on top. No
   rewriting, copying or reformatting.
2. **The "why" up front.** Why this investor specifically, for example a past investment or the
   fund's stage and focus.
3. **Who the founder is and what they are working on.** The problem solved and for what
   customer, plus traction, so the meeting looks worth the investor's time. If fundraising,
   link a short deck so the investor can check stage and thesis fit quickly.

Template:

```
Subject: Intro Request: [Your Name] <> [Recipient's Name]

Hi [Introducer's Name],

Thanks so much for offering to connect me with [Recipient's Name]!

Here's some quick context for them:

I'm the founder of [Company Name], which is [1-sentence description of what you do and for
who]. [1-2 sentences or 3 bullet points with your traction].

I'm hoping to chat with you about [specific reason: advice, feedback, investment, etc.], because
[why this person specifically].

Happy to work around your schedule. Feel free to share a link to your calendar, or if it's
easier mine is here: [link]. [Short deck: link]

Thanks for the consideration!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

Tell the founder to avoid three mistakes:

- **Replying on the existing thread.** Send the forwardable email as a new, clean email, then
  reply on the original thread to say it has been sent. The exception is when the introducer
  asked for a blurb in the thread.
- **Making it long or vague.** If the investor has to hunt for the ask, they will ignore it.
  Keep it to a few short paragraphs.
- **Not following up.** If there is no word after about 10 days, send the introducer one nudge.
  One, not several.

One note per investor. Do not suggest an intro through someone who barely knows the investor.

**The one-sentence test.** For every investor on the list, the founder must be able to finish
this sentence: "I think [name] will care because ___." If they can't, research more or drop the
investor.

## Step 5: Brainstorm channels to test

This step is a brainstorm with the founder, not a recommendation from you. These are channels to
test for investors with no warm path. The founder chooses. Your job is to help them think it
through.

Run it in three short turns. Do not skip ahead or combine them.

**Turn 1: ask, and wait for answers.** Ask only these, in one short message:
- Where are your target investors visibly active (posting, replying, speaking, hosting)?
- Where are *you* strongest: writing, video, in person, building in public?
- How many hours a week can you give this?
- Any events in the next 60 days where several targets will be in the room?
- Any ideas of your own for reaching them that you want to try?

Do not propose channels in this turn.

**Turn 2: lay out the options and react together.** Show the full list below, with a one-line
read on each for this founder based on their answers (fit, effort, speed to a signal). Include
any idea the founder raised. Then ask which ones they want to test and whether anything is
missing. Do not pick for them.

| Channel | Works best when | Watch out for |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| Cold email | You have a clear traction line and can find a direct address | Long emails; attaching a full deck first |
| LinkedIn DM | The investor is active there and your profile is strong | Your profile is the next click. Fix it first |
| Engage with their content first, then message | You have 2-4 weeks before you need a reply | Generic comments. Add a real point of view |
| The investor's own intake form or "pitch us" page | The firm says it reads them | Treating it as lesser. Write it with the same care |
| In-person events | Targets attend, and you are better in a room | Pitching on the spot. Aim for a follow-up, not a decision |
| Short personalized video message (1-2 minutes) | You are natural on camera and can reference something you share with that investor | Reusing one generic recording for everyone |
| Other platforms the investor uses (X, Instagram, TikTok) | They are more active and responsive there than on LinkedIn or email | Assuming they read DMs there. Check how they engage first |
| Regular community update | You are 6+ months from raising | Letting it lapse after two sends |
| Something creative and specific to this investor | You have a real hook only you could use | Gimmicks with no substance behind them |

A teaser deck or demo video is not a channel. It is an asset the founder can attach on any of
these (see Step 6).

If they ask what you would choose, give your view in two or three sentences and say why, then
hand the decision back.

**Turn 3: confirm the test.** Once the founder has picked (steer them to 2-3, since they still
have a company to build), write a one-line hypothesis for each, for example: "Investors who ran
repair shops will reply to a LinkedIn DM that leads with my 10 years in the industry." Then plan
the touch points. Many investors will not act on a single cold message, so for each chosen
channel sketch a short sequence of two or three touches across the batch (for example: comment
on a post, then a DM, then a progress update), without repeating the same message. Then move to
Step 6 for those channels only.

---

## Step 6: Draft messaging for the chosen channels

Write messaging only for the channels the founder chose in Step 5, shaped for each one:

| Channel | What to write |
|---------|---------------|
| Cold email | Subject line that carries the strongest fact, then 100-150 words, one link |
| LinkedIn DM | 60-100 words, no attachments, the reason for this investor in the first line |
| Engage first, then message | 2-3 comment ideas with a real point of view, then a DM that refers back to the exchange |
| Intake form | Direct answers to the form's own questions, same five ingredients, no filler |
| In-person events | A pre-event note asking for 15 minutes, a two-sentence spoken opener, and a next-day follow-up |
| Personalized video | A 150-250 word talking outline (not a script to read), plus the two-line note that goes with the link |
| X, Instagram, TikTok | A reply or comment idea first, then a DM of 50 words or fewer that points to one link |
| Community update | A short invitation to receive updates, plus the first update |
| Creative | Help the founder shape their idea, and make sure the five ingredients are still in it |

Every first message contains five ingredients. Keep it readable in under a minute.

1. **Why this investor.** The specific reason, from the one-sentence test. Put it first.
2. **Why you.** The most impressive, relevant fact about the founder or team. Critical when
   there is little traction.
3. **What you are building and for who.** One plain sentence. No buzzwords or acronyms.
4. **Traction.** The 1-3 strongest data points, with their time frame.
5. **The ask.** Raising, a specific question, or building a relationship ahead of a raise. One
   ask, easy to answer.

**Rules for the draft:**

- **Lead with the strongest thing.** Not chronological order. If the best fact is the growth
  number, the 15 years in the industry, or the prior exit, it goes in the first two lines.
- **Personalize with a reason to meet, not proof of research.** "I saw you invest in early-stage
  SaaS" ❌. A specific thing they said or did, tied to why this company matters to them ✅.
- **Offer one way to learn more:** a 5-7 slide teaser built to be read in two minutes, or a
  60-second demo. Not the full deck. If the `deck-review` skill from this toolkit is installed,
  suggest running it on the deck before anything is sent.
- **Sound like the founder.** Match how they write. Cut compliments and filler. Tell them to
  read and edit every message before sending. Generic, flattering, machine-sounding messages
  are easy to spot.
- **Pre-revenue variant.** Replace traction with founder-market fit and customer demand:
  interviews run, commitments made, design partners signed, how fast they have moved. The ask
  can be a specific question the investor is unusually well placed to answer.

**Shape of a good message (invented example):**

```
Hi [Name], saw your [specific post/investment] on [topic], and you [relevant experience].

I spent [N years doing X] and am now building [plain one-sentence description] for [customer].

We launched [time frame] ago and have:
- [data point with context]
- [data point with context]

We're raising [amount / round]. If it sounds relevant, send me a time that works and I'll book it.
```

Write one draft per chosen channel and investor group, then personalize the first two lines for
each individual. Show one draft at a time when there are several, so the founder can react.
Leave `[TK]` wherever a fact is missing.

---

## Step 7: Check before sending

Mark each ✅ or 🔴. Fix every 🔴 before the founder sends.

- [ ] This investor actually invests in companies like this, at this stage.
- [ ] There is a specific reason for reaching out to this individual.
- [ ] Someone unfamiliar with the company can understand what it does from one sentence.
- [ ] The most compelling thing about the team or traction is highlighted, near the top.
- [ ] Relevant numbers are included, with context.
- [ ] The message is short, easy to skim, and sounds like the founder.
- [ ] It is easy for the investor to learn more.
- [ ] There is one clear ask.

**Honesty check.** Mark 🔴 and rewrite if the message:
- calls a waitlist "customers", or unsigned pipeline "revenue"
- states a number without its time frame, or rounds it up
- implies an investor, customer or partner commitment that is not signed
- contains any fact the founder did not give you

Credibility is what gets an investor to yes. They will ask questions.

**Profile check** (for LinkedIn outreach): the startup is listed, the profile says what it does,
the experience shows why this founder, and co-founders are easy to find.

## Step 8: Run it as an experiment

**Set up the first batch: 30 investors in three groups.**
- 10 who have personally experienced the problem
- 10 who have invested in similar or complementary companies
- 10 whose broader thesis fits

Assign each group a message and a channel from Step 5. Investors with a warm path go through
the intro instead and are tracked separately.

**Create a tracker** with these columns. If you can create files, make it a spreadsheet file
(.xlsx or .csv) pre-filled with every investor named so far. Otherwise output a table the
founder can paste into a spreadsheet.

| Investor | Firm | Group | Why they're a fit | Warm path or cold | Channel | Date contacted | Response | Meeting booked? | Feedback / learnings | Next step |
|----------|------|-------|-------------------|-------------------|---------|----------------|----------|-----------------|----------------------|-----------|

**Follow-ups.** No reply does not mean no. Draft follow-ups that give the investor a reason to
reconsider:

- "Just bumping this to the top of your inbox" ❌
- A real update since the first message: new customers, a revenue milestone, a retention signal,
  design partners converting, a launch ✅

If nothing meaningful has changed, advise waiting until it has. After a couple of unanswered
attempts, move on and revisit later. If an investor says "too early", suggest asking whether
they would like to receive the founder's regular updates. If the `investor-update` skill from
this toolkit is installed, offer to draft the first one with it.

**Review after the batch.** Ask:
- Which group replied most?
- Which channel produced replies, and which produced meetings?
- Which traction point or opening line landed?
- Are these investors really investing at this stage?
- Do replies show they understood what the company does?
- Is the same objection coming up repeatedly?

Then adjust the list, the message and the channels for the next batch. Be clear that 30 is too
small for statistical conclusions. It is enough to spot patterns and stop wasting time.

Before a large send, suggest getting feedback on the list and the message from a founder who has
raised, or from an existing investor.

## Deliverables

Depending on what the founder asked for, output some or all of:

1. **Readiness read**: one short paragraph, honest.
2. **Ideal investor persona**
3. **Channel plan**: the 2-3 channels the founder chose, each with its hypothesis
4. **Messaging**: drafts for each chosen channel, with per-investor opening lines
5. **Checklist result**: ✅ / 🔴 per item
6. **Tracker**: the table, pre-filled with any investors named
7. **Follow-up drafts**, when there is real progress to report

## Tone guidance

- **Candid.** If cold outreach is the wrong use of the founder's time right now, say so and say
  what to do instead.
- **Short.** Drafts that take more than a minute to read get cut.
- **Founder's voice.** Don't impose a corporate tone.
- **Practical.** Every suggestion is something the founder can do this week.

## What this skill does NOT do

- It does not send messages or contact investors. It produces drafts for the founder to review,
  edit and send.
- It does not invent traction, credentials, or facts about an investor. Unknowns stay `[TK]`.
- It does not write mass, unpersonalized blasts. Every message names a reason for that investor.
- It does not give legal, tax or securities advice. Founders should consult professionals on
  how they are permitted to solicit investment.
